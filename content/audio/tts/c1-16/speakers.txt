G: Everyone assumes I took up pottery to cope with stress at work, but actually, what got me started was a smell. I walked past a studio one evening, and the smell of wet clay took me straight back to my grandmother's garden shed, where I used to spend whole summers making lopsided pots. I signed up that same week. Not until I'd ruined about forty bowls did I realise that every collapse was teaching me something, about pressure, about speed, about when to stop. These days, when a pot goes wrong, I'm almost pleased. It's the failures I've learnt the most from.

B: My sister was expecting her first baby, and I'd decided, rather rashly, that I'd knit her a blanket. Never before had I held a pair of knitting needles. People warned me it would take endless patience, and it does take some, but that's not what struck me. What I hadn't expected was the social side. The group I joined meets in the back room of a pub every Tuesday, and far from being a quiet, solitary hobby, it's the loudest evening of my week. I've made more friends there in two years than I did in ten at work.
D: Speaker three.
E: It was my physiotherapist who suggested woodturning, after I'd had an operation on my shoulder. She said I needed something that would rebuild strength gradually, and, moreover, something I'd actually want to do every day. The thing it's changed most, oddly, is how I shop. Having made a salad bowl myself, I just can't bring myself to pay three dollars for a mass-produced one. Conversely, I'll now happily spend a small fortune on something handmade, because I know exactly how many hours went into it. My husband thinks it's a very expensive kind of therapy.

H: I spend ten hours a day in front of a screen, so what I wanted was something with no undo button. Calligraphy seemed perfect. I didn't go into it to meet people. In fact, I practise alone at the kitchen table. Little did I know how slow it would be, though. Only after a full year could I write my own name without wincing. Some people in my online forum say it's improved their concentration at work. I can't honestly say that's been the case for me. What it has taught me is to wait, and to keep going when nothing seems to change.

C: A friend gave me a bookbinding kit for my fortieth birthday, as a joke, I think, because I'd once said I'd love to make my own notebooks.
