C: You will hear five short extracts in which people talk about getting rid of most of their possessions.
C: Speaker one.
A: When my grandmother died, my mother and I spent the whole of one summer clearing her house. Every drawer and every cupboard was full, and most of it, honestly, she hadn't touched in decades. I came home, looked round my own flat and thought, I'm doing exactly the same thing. So I started. I gave away about two-thirds of my clothes and nearly all of my books. What I hadn't expected was the reaction at work. A couple of colleagues seemed almost offended, as if I were judging them. One of them still makes little comments about it.
C: Speaker two.
F: Our landlord sold the house, and the only place we could find near the kids' school was an apartment about half the size. So it wasn't a choice. There simply wasn't room for most of what we owned. I was dreading it. But a year later, I can't name more than a handful of things we got rid of. All those boxes, and I couldn't tell you what was in any of them. I'd assumed I'd miss far more than I do. Much less, really, than I'd miss a single afternoon at the beach.
C: Speaker three.
G: Mine started as a bet. A friend of mine said I couldn't go a whole year without buying any new clothes, and I said I could do better than that. I'd get rid of everything I hadn't worn in the past twelve months as well. Neither of us thought I'd stick to it. The odd thing is how much time it's given me. I used to spend half of every Saturday in shops, and most evenings scrolling through websites. Now I've got whole weekends free, and I've taken up climbing. Each of those hours had been going somewhere, and I'd never even noticed.
C: Speaker four.
D: We were burgled two years ago. They took very little, as it turned out, because there was very little worth taking. But the police asked us to list everything we owned, and we couldn't. That shook me. So I went through every room and sold whatever had any value at all. I'd assumed I'd make a few hundred pounds. In the end it came to over four thousand, which paid for a family holiday. I'm still slightly embarrassed by how much money we'd had sitting in the loft.
C: Speaker five.
E: I'd been reading a lot about waste, how much of what we buy ends up in landfill within a year, and I felt I had to do something rather than just worry about it. So I gave most of my things to thrift stores, and these days I repair what I've got instead of replacing it. I thought it would be a rather lonely sort of project. But my teenage daughter, of all people, got involved. She now sells her old clothes online and lectures me about fast fashion. And my husband, who has never thrown anything away in his life, has cleared out half the garage.
