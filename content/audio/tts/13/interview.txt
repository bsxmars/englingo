F: Hello and welcome to Second Wind, the programme about people who took up something new later in life. My guest today is Rachel Moore, who started swimming in the sea at forty-five, and last summer swam the twenty-one miles across the English Channel. Rachel, how did it all start?
C: With a bad knee, actually. I'd been a runner for years, and when the doctor told me to give it up, I was devastated. A friend kept going on about how good cold water was for you, so in the end I gave in and went with her one morning in March.
F: In March! What was that like?
C: Awful, to be honest. I lasted about ninety seconds. But I came out feeling more awake than I had in months, and I couldn't stop smiling. So I went back the next day, and the day after that.
F: Did your family support you?
C: My husband thought it was a phase and that I'd get over it by the summer. It was my daughter who talked me into joining a club. She said if I was going to do something that dangerous, I should at least do it with people who knew what they were doing.
F: So when did the Channel come into it?
C: Not for a long time. Someone at the club brought it up as a joke, and I just laughed it off. But the idea stayed with me. It took me three years to build up to it. I had to cut down on sugar, put on a bit of weight, which you need for the cold, and get used to swimming for ten hours at a time.
F: Was there a moment when you nearly gave up?
C: Oh, plenty. The worst was two months before the swim, when I picked up a shoulder injury. I seriously thought about calling the whole thing off. My coach was brilliant, though. She worked out a completely new training plan and wouldn't let me feel sorry for myself.
F: And the swim itself?
C: Fifteen hours and twelve minutes. The first half went fine, but around hour nine the tide turned and I hardly moved for an hour. What kept me going was my daughter on the boat. Every thirty minutes she held up a sign with a terrible joke on it.
F: What would you say to listeners who'd like to try something like this?
C: Don't put it off until you feel ready, because you never will. Find people who'll look out for you, start small, and don't compare yourself with anyone else.
F: Rachel Moore, thank you so much.
