D: Good afternoon, and welcome to the Lost Property Office. I'm Martin, and I'll be showing you round for the next forty minutes or so. Before we start, could I ask you to keep your bags with you at all times? It would be rather embarrassing if any of them ended up on our shelves.
D: This office first opened in nineteen thirty-three, and it's been here, underneath the main station, since the nineteen fifties. Every year we receive around three hundred thousand items, mostly left on trains and buses and in taxis. Apart from umbrellas, which arrive by the hundred every wet Monday, the most common item is, perhaps unsurprisingly, mobile phones. They overtook keys about fifteen years ago.
D: Everything that comes in is logged on our database within twenty-four hours, and we hold it for three months. If nobody has claimed it by then, it's either given to charity or, in the case of electronics, sold at auction. People often ask why we don't keep things for longer. The simple answer is space. Even with the shelves going right up to the ceiling, we'd run out of room within a matter of weeks.
D: Now, as we walk along this corridor, you'll see some of the stranger things we've had in over the years. Somebody did come for the dress, by the way, though not until the following Thursday. We never found out whether the wedding went ahead.
