H: In 1931 the mill was badly damaged by a fire, which is believed to have started in the drying room. Nobody was killed, thankfully, but the roof was completely destroyed, and the mill was never used for flour again.

H: In 1985 the mill was bought by the county council, and the restoration began. The new roof was made from oak, using the same methods as the original. Each of the sixty-four windows had to be made by hand. The whole project took eleven years, and the mill was finally opened to the public in 1996.
H: The restoration cost around four million euros, and a surprising amount of that money was raised by local people, through concerts, quiz nights, even a sponsored swim across the river.
