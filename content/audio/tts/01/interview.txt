A: Welcome back to Weekend Lives. My guest today is Daniel Price, who spent a whole year without a smartphone. Daniel, what made you do it?
D: Well, it wasn't a big plan. I'd been working as a sales manager for about ten years, and I used to check my phone the moment I woke up. One morning I realised I'd read forty messages before I'd even had a coffee. That was the moment.
A: So you just switched it off?
D: Not straight away. I'd tried to cut down before, several times, and it had never worked. This time I gave the phone to my sister and bought a really old mobile that could only make calls.
A: How did the people around you react?
D: My colleagues thought I'd gone mad! My boss was worried at first, because clients used to text me late at night. But after a month or so he admitted that I'd been answering emails much more carefully.
A: What was the hardest part?
D: Honestly, maps. I've never had a good sense of direction. In the first week I got lost three times. These days I actually look at street names, and I've started to enjoy finding my way.
A: And what has changed most?
D: Sleep, definitely. I've been sleeping about an hour longer every night. And I read again. When I was a teenager I would read a book a week, and then I just stopped. This year I've finished twenty-two novels.
A: The year is over now. Have you gone back to a smartphone?
D: I have, but with rules. It stays in the kitchen at night, and I've deleted every social media app. I'm not against technology. I just don't want it to decide how I spend my mornings.
A: Daniel Price, thank you very much.
