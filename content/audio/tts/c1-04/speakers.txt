A: You will hear five short extracts in which people talk about building work they had done on their homes.
A: Speaker one.
E: We had the kitchen redone last year. It was supposed to have been finished in six weeks, and in the end it took five months. Every time I rang, I was told the units were being delivered next week. Always next week. To be fair, the work itself is beautiful. It's been admired by everyone who's seen it. But if I were doing it again, I'd want every single date written into the contract, with a penalty if it's missed. A handshake and a promise just aren't enough. Not when people are being paid as much as that.
A: Speaker two.
B: Our roof needed replacing, so we got three builders round to give us a price and went with the middle one. The price never changed, which I'd been warned it might. The problem was next door. Scaffolding was put up against our neighbour's wall without anyone asking her, and a couple of her roof tiles got broken in the process. The builders paid for the repair, eventually, but she's barely spoken to us since. Next time, I'd knock on her door before a single pole went up. It would have cost me five minutes and a cup of tea.
A: Speaker three.
G: We'd been told the bathroom would cost about eight thousand pounds. By the time it was finished, we'd been charged nearly fourteen. Every extra was explained very reasonably. The floor turned out to be rotten, the pipes had to be moved, and so on. I don't think we were cheated, exactly. But we hadn't put anything aside for surprises, and we had to borrow from my parents. Honestly, I'd add a third to whatever figure you're given. Old houses keep secrets, and they're always revealed at the worst possible moment.
A: Speaker four.
H: I had an extension built while I was working abroad, which, looking back, was asking for trouble. Photos were sent to me every week, and everything looked fine on a screen. It was only when I got home that I saw the tiling was crooked and the plastering was frankly amateur. Half of it has had to be redone. The firm had been recommended by a friend, so I hadn't bothered checking. If I ever have work done again, I'll be there in person, even if it means taking unpaid leave. Things get done differently when someone's watching.
A: Speaker five.
C: Our builder was charming, quick and cheap, and halfway through the job he simply stopped answering his phone. We later found out he'd been taken to court by two other clients. If we'd looked him up online, or asked to see a job he'd finished, we'd have known in five minutes. We ended up getting another firm in to finish the work, and it cost us far more than it should have. I'm not a suspicious person by nature, but I've learnt to be. These days I'd want to be shown references before anyone so much as picked up a hammer.
