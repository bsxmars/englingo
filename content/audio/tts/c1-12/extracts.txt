H: Extract three. You hear a student talking to her tutor about her dissertation.
D: So, how's the dissertation coming along?
E: Slowly. I've collected all the data, but I'm not sure what it shows. Half the results suggest that the programme worked, and the other half suggest it made no difference at all.
D: That's not necessarily a problem. Whether a programme works often depends on who it's for. Have you looked at the age groups separately?
E: No. I hadn't thought of that.
D: I'd suggest you start there. And it's essential that you state clearly in your introduction what you're not claiming. Examiners are far more impressed by a modest conclusion that's well supported than by an ambitious one that isn't.
E: That's a relief, honestly. I thought I'd have to prove something dramatic.
D: Very few dissertations do. What matters is that you show you understand the limits of your data.
